Will: <short pause> Yes. There is.
Hannah (laughing): Get out.
Will (laughing): No, honestly! It's the postal rule. If post is a sensible way to accept, the acceptance takes effect the moment the letter's posted. Not when it lands. When it goes in the box.
Hannah (thinking aloud): Hang on, that's bonkers. So the contract exists while the letter's just... sat in a sorting office in Swindon.
Will: Sat in Swindon. Yes. And the law basically shrugs and says, well, you did everything you could. <laugh>
Hannah (sceptical): Okay. And the normal rule is what, then?
Will (brisk): The normal rule is that acceptance has to actually reach the person who made the offer. Silence doesn't count. A letter in your drawer doesn't count.
Hannah (dry): So I can't accept by thinking about it really hard.
Will: Sadly not. But you can switch the postal rule off. If my offer says, "your acceptance must reach me", then posting isn't enough.
Hannah (relieved): Oh, that's fair. So it's only the default.
Will: Only the default, yes. And here's the sting. If I want to take my offer back, that only works once you actually receive my letter. Posting it doesn't help me.
Hannah (mock-outraged): So posting helps the person accepting, but never the person withdrawing?
Will (amused): <short pause> Exactly that.
