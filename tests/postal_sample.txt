## Rule
Will: Welcome back. Today, Hannah, we're doing one of the great dramas of contract law.
Hannah: Is it a drama, though, Will? It's letters.
Will: It's letters, a van, and a man who changes his mind. <laugh> Acceptance: when does it actually take effect?
Hannah: Go on then. Start me from scratch.
Will: The basic rule: acceptance only counts once it's communicated to the person who made the offer. Silence doesn't do it. Neither does a letter sitting in your drawer.
Hannah: Fair. You can't accept by thinking about it really hard.
Will: Sadly not. But there's one big exception: the postal rule. Where it's reasonable to accept by post, acceptance takes effect the moment the letter is posted, as long as it's properly stamped and addressed.
Hannah: Posted, not received?
Will: Posted. That's the rule from Adams and Lindsell.
Hannah: And if the letter gets lost?
Will: Still a contract. That's the Household Fire case.
Hannah: So the contract exists while the letter is stuck in a sorting office in Swindon.
Will: Exactly. Royal Mail holds your contract hostage, but it's still a contract.
Hannah: And the other way round? Can you withdraw an offer by post too?
Will: You can try, but withdrawing an offer only counts when it actually reaches the other side. Posting it isn't enough.
Hannah: So acceptance: on posting. Withdrawal: on arrival.
## Exam mode
Will: That's the whole topic in a sentence. Now, how would the examiners get us?
Hannah: Ooh. Exam mode.
Will: Exam mode. Ash offers to sell Ben his van, by letter. On Monday, Ben posts his acceptance. On Tuesday, Ash posts a letter withdrawing the offer, and it arrives on Wednesday. Ben's acceptance only arrives on Thursday. Is there a contract?
Hannah: <short pause> No. Ash's withdrawal got there first.
Will: And that is exactly the answer they want you to pick.
Hannah: Oh no.
Will: The contract was made on Monday, when Ben posted his acceptance. By Tuesday, there was nothing left to withdraw.
Hannah: So it's not a race to the letterbox.
Will: Not once acceptance is posted. The deciding fact is the posting date. Find it first.
Hannah: Right. What else do they hide?
Will: Two classics. First, the offer's own wording. If Ash had said, "your acceptance must reach me", the postal rule is switched off, and acceptance only counts when it arrives.
Hannah: So read the offer like a suspicious landlord.
Will: <laugh> Always. Second, email. People assume the postal rule covers it.
Hannah: I would. It's basically a letter.
Will: It's not treated like one. A phone acceptance counts when it's heard, and email is generally treated the same way: when it's received.
## Recap
Hannah: Recap, then?
Will: Acceptance by post: on posting, even if it's lost. Withdrawing an offer: on arrival. Phone or email: on receipt.
Hannah: And if the offer says acceptance must reach them?
Will: Then it must reach them.
