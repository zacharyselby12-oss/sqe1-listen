---
title: Contract WS01 · Postal rule (style sample only)
recap:
- A posted acceptance takes effect when it is posted, even if the letter is lost.
- An offer can switch the postal rule off by requiring acceptance to reach the offeror.
- Withdrawing an offer only works when the withdrawal reaches the other person.
invented:
- Swindon
- Ash
- Ben
- Monday
- Tuesday
- Wednesday

## Rule
Will (warm): Right. Hannah. Quick one before we start.
Hannah (wary): Oh no. Is this a trick one?
Will (amused): A little bit. Say you post a letter accepting my offer, and the post office loses it, so it never reaches me. Is there a contract?
Hannah (incredulous): No! Surely not. I never even told you!
Will: <short pause> Yes. There is.
Hannah (laughing): Get out.
Will (laughing): No, honestly! It's the postal rule. As long as post is a sensible way to accept, your acceptance takes effect the moment you post the letter, not when it arrives.
Hannah (thinking aloud): Hang on, that's bonkers. So the contract exists while the letter's just... sat in a sorting office in Swindon.
Will: Sat in Swindon. Yes. And the law basically shrugs and says, well, you did everything you could. <laugh>
Hannah (sceptical): Okay. And the normal rule is what, then?
Will (brisk): Normally an acceptance only counts once it actually reaches the person who made the offer, which is why staying silent doesn't count, and neither does a letter still sitting in your drawer.
Hannah (dry): So I can't accept by thinking about it really hard.
Will: Sadly not. But you can switch the postal rule off. If my offer says, "your acceptance must reach me", then posting isn't enough.
Hannah (relieved): Oh, that's fair. So it's only the default.
Will: It's only the default, yes. And here's the sting: if I want to take my offer back, that only works once you actually receive my withdrawal, so posting it doesn't help me at all.
Hannah (mock-outraged): So posting helps the person accepting, but never the person withdrawing?
Will (amused): <short pause> Exactly that.
## Exam mode
Will (brisk): Okay, exam hat on. Ash posts a letter accepting Ben's offer on Monday, Ben rings him on Tuesday to withdraw it, and the letter only arrives on Wednesday. Is there a contract?
Hannah (thinking aloud): Hmm. Ben withdrew first. So... no? He got in before the letter arrived.
Will: That's the trap. Ben's withdrawal only works when it reaches Ash, which is Tuesday, but Ash accepted on Monday, the moment he posted the letter, so by Tuesday there's already a contract.
Hannah (groaning): Ugh. I always want the arrival to count.
Will (dry): Everyone does. You only need two dates: when the acceptance was posted and when the withdrawal arrived. If the posting came first, there's a contract.
Hannah: And what if Ben's offer had said "acceptance must reach me"?
Will (pleased): Ooh, good. Then posting isn't enough. The postal rule's off, so the acceptance only counts when the letter reaches Ben on Wednesday.
Hannah (catching on): And by then the phone call on Tuesday has already withdrawn the offer.
Will: So, no contract. One sentence in the offer flips the whole answer.
Hannah (thoughtful): Right. I'll be reading the offer for that sentence first.
## Recap
Will (warm): Three things to take away.
Hannah: One. A posted acceptance works on posting, even if the letter's lost.
Will: Two. An offer can switch that off, by saying the acceptance has to reach the person.
Hannah (brisk): Three. Withdrawing an offer only works once the withdrawal reaches the other side.
